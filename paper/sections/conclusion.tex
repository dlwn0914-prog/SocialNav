\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We separated what a crowd-navigation robot can do -- safe paths decoded from a codebook of human walking behaviours -- from which of them a
particular user wants, and learned the latter from a handful of A/B questions. Five EUBO queries cut the simple regret on unseen situations
from that of the human-data prior (0.45--0.47) to 0.09--0.12. Choosing the questions with the posterior, by EUBO or by information gain, matters more than the estimator, and the candidate set bounds
what any preference can achieve. Next, the user study of Sec.~\ref{sec:pilot} will test whether real people differ enough that a
personal $\hat\theta$ predicts their held-out answers better than a shared one, and closed-loop tests with reactive pedestrians and a learned
predictor will show what the preference changes once the crowd responds to the robot.
